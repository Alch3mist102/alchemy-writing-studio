Grammar: A Concise Handbook

Spelled with two m's and an a—never "gammer." Master spelling, punctuation, and sentence architecture.

Chapter I: Top 100 Most Misspelled Words
Chapter II: Punctuation & Sentence Boundaries
1. Oxford (Serial) Comma: Place a comma before and/or in lists of three or more items ("ghosts, ghouls, and shadows").
2. FANBOYS + Complete Sentences: Use a comma before for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so only when joining two independent clauses.
3. Comma Splices & Run-Ons: Never join two complete sentences with only a comma ("The door slammed, she ran"). Fix with a period, a semicolon, or a comma + FANBOYS.
4. Introductory Clauses: Put a comma after an opening dependent phrase ("When the lantern flickered, she froze.").
5. Nonessential Appositives: Wrap removable extra details in commas ("The relic, forged in bog-iron, hummed.").
6. Dialogue Tags vs. Action Beats: Use a comma for speech tags ("Run," he whispered.) and a period for action beats ("Run." He drew his blade.).
7. Semicolons (;) & Colons (:): Semicolons link two closely related complete sentences; colons follow a complete sentence to introduce a list, quote, or explanation.
Chapter III: Agreement, Modifiers & Structure
8. Subject-Verb Agreement: Match the verb to the true subject, ignoring prepositional phrases in between ("The chest of ancient coins is heavy," not "are").
9. Pronoun Case & Agreement: Use subject pronouns (I, he, she, we, they, who) for actors and object pronouns (me, him, her, us, them, whom) after verbs or prepositions ("between you and me").
10. Dangling & Misplaced Modifiers: Place descriptive phrases directly beside the noun they modify ("Covered in dust, the tome sat on the desk," not "Covered in dust, he found the tome").
11. Parallel Structure (Parallelism): Keep items in lists or comparisons in the same grammatical form ("She loved reading lore, brewing tonics, and exploring ruins").
12. Apostrophes & Homophones: Use apostrophes for contractions (it's = it is) and possession (the guild's seal / the writers' desks)—never for simple plurals.
